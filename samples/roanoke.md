# The Lost Colony of Roanoke Explained

## The Empty Village
card: The Empty Village
visuals: Roanoke colony engraving | John White watercolor | Theodor de Bry Virginia

> Wide shot of a small wooden rowboat gliding through thick morning mist toward a dark forested island, 1590
August, 1590. A small boat pushes through the mist toward a quiet island off the coast of what is now North Carolina.
> Close up of a grey bearded Elizabethan man in a worn doublet, John White, staring at the shore with hope in his eyes
John White has waited three long years for this moment. His daughter is here. So is his granddaughter, Virginia Dare, the first English child born in the Americas.

> clip: forest edge beach waves
His men sound a trumpet. They sing old English songs into the trees. No one answers.
> Over the shoulder of John White looking at an empty clearing with a tall wooden palisade fence, no houses left
The houses are gone. The fence still stands...
> Close up of a weathered wooden post with letters carved deep into the bark, moody light
and on one of its posts, someone has carved a single word. Croatoan.
> clip: island aerial sunset
So where did one hundred and fifteen people go?

## Why He Left
card: Why He Left
visuals: Spanish Armada painting | Elizabethan galleon | Walter Raleigh portrait

> A small group of hungry colonists in Elizabethan clothes gathered around John White on a beach, pleading
To answer that, we have to go back to 1587. The colony was short on food and supplies, so the settlers begged their governor, John White, to sail home to England for help.
> John White on the deck of a ship looking back at the shrinking shoreline
He planned to be gone a few months.
> clip: stormy sea waves
Then history got in the way. Spain sent its great Armada against England, and every good ship was ordered to stay and fight.

> John White alone in a candle lit English room, looking at a map of the American coast
Think about that for a second. A grandfather, stuck on the wrong side of an ocean, while his family waited for him.

## The Clue in the Trees
card: The Clue In The Trees
visuals: Algonquian village watercolor | Outer Banks old map | Secotan village

> John White and colonists agreeing on a plan, one man pointing at a carved tree trunk
Before White left, they made a deal. If the colonists moved, they would carve where they went. If they were in danger, they would add a cross.
> Close up of the carved post again, clean letters and no cross anywhere
There was no cross.
> Wide shot of a peaceful coastal Native Algonquian village of long houses by the sea
And Croatoan was the name of a friendly island to the south, home to a people who had helped them before.

> English settlers and Native Croatoan people sharing food around a fire in a village
One leading theory says the settlers simply joined them.
> clip: archaeologist excavation
Archaeologists on Hatteras Island have dug up English objects mixed into a Native village.
> The island at sunset seen from the sea, warm golden light over the trees
Maybe the Lost Colony was never lost at all... just never found.
